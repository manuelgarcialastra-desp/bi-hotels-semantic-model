# AI Impact on P&L — Revenue Team

## The argument that doesn't work

"We do the same things faster." That's efficiency. Your manager already assumes it. It doesn't move the P&L conversation.

---

## Three arguments that do

### 1. Full coverage → decisions on facts, not samples

Without AI, the team analyzes samples. With AI, the team analyzes the full universe.

The **tipificador** is the clearest example: classifying root cause for every single pricepoint was operationally impossible before. Now Sourcing can be told exactly which providers are losing on which hotels and why. That turns a broad negotiation into a targeted one. The delta between a well-directed negotiation and a generic one has direct margin impact.

### 2. Detection speed → less time bleeding

The gap between "there is a competitiveness problem" and "we understand the root cause and act" is where revenue is lost. If a SINAI rule is misconfigured, every day it goes undetected is pricepoints losing unnecessarily.

Crawler projects and adversity analysis compress that gap. **Time-to-action on a pricing problem has direct financial value**, not just operational value.

### 3. Capabilities that didn't exist before

Some projects don't speed up existing work — they create work that was never possible.

The tipificador didn't exist. The second brain as a queryable knowledge system didn't exist. NCS analysis at the granularity the team achieved didn't exist.

These new capabilities unlock commercial levers — provider negotiations, hotel prioritization, SINAI rule optimization — that were previously blind.

---

## How to quantify it

```
Δ adversity × search volume × conversion rate × average ticket = revenue impact
```

You don't need the exact number in the room. You need to show the path exists and that every component is measurable with data you already have.

---

## The one-line for your manager

> "AI doesn't just make us faster — it gives us access to analysis that was previously impossible at scale. That means pricing, negotiation, and SINAI rule decisions are made on complete facts instead of samples. In a business where each point of adversity has direct margin impact, that's P&L, not efficiency."
