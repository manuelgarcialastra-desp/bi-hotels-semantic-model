The argument that doesn't work

Wrong frame

"We do the same things faster."

This is efficiency. Your manager already assumes it. It doesn't move the P&L conversation.

Right frame

"We do things that were previously impossible — and that has direct margin impact."

01

Full coverage → decisions
on facts, not samples

Scale that was operationally impossible before

Without AI, the team analyzes samples. With AI, the team analyzes the full universe of pricepoints, every Tuesday, automatically.

That means decisions about provider negotiations, SINAI rules, and hotel prioritization are made on complete data — not on representative subsets that may miss tail cases.

🎯
Tipificador Classifying root cause for every single pricepoint was operationally impossible before. Now Sourcing knows exactly which providers are losing on which hotels and why — turning a broad negotiation into a targeted one. The delta has direct margin impact.
02

Detection speed →
less time bleeding

Time-to-action on pricing problems has financial value

The gap between "there is a competitiveness problem" and "we understand the root cause and act" is where revenue is lost.

If a SINAI rule is misconfigured, every day it goes undetected means pricepoints losing unnecessarily. Faster detection → faster correction → less margin erosion.

📡
Crawlers + Adversity Analysis These projects compress the detection-to-action gap. That compression is not just an operational improvement — it has a direct, quantifiable revenue effect proportional to the volume of affected pricepoints.
03

New capabilities that
didn't exist before

Not acceleration — creation of entirely new levers

Some projects don't speed up existing work. They create work that was never possible.

These new capabilities unlock commercial levers — provider negotiations, hotel prioritization, rule optimization — that were previously blind. Blind not because of lack of effort, but because of scale.

🧠
Second Brain  ·  NCS Analysis  ·  Tipificador None of these existed before. Each one opens a decision surface that previously required either a dedicated team or didn't happen. That's not efficiency — that's new P&L surface.

How to quantify it

You don't need the exact number in the room. You need to show the path exists and every component is measurable with data you already have.

Δ adversity × search volume × conversion rate × average ticket = revenue impact
Δ adversity is the improvement driven by better decisions made possible by AI analysis.
Every 0.1pp reduction in adversity, at Despegar's search volume, is a measurable revenue delta.
Δ time-to-action × affected pricepoints × weight × daily volume = bleed avoided
Δ time-to-action is the days saved between detecting and correcting a pricing issue.
Quantifiable directly from incident history and pricepoint data.

The three P&L arguments

  • 01
    Full coverage Decisions on every pricepoint — not on samples. Enables targeted negotiations and rule corrections with complete information.
  • 02
    Detection speed Compressed gap between problem and action. Every day saved is quantifiable revenue from pricepoints that stop losing unnecessarily.
  • 03
    New capabilities Tipificador, NCS analysis, second brain — none existed before. Each unlocks a commercial lever that was previously blind or inaccessible at scale.

The one-liner for your manager

"AI doesn't just make us faster — it gives us access to analysis that was previously impossible at scale. That means pricing, negotiation, and SINAI rule decisions are made on complete facts instead of samples. In a business where each point of adversity has direct margin impact, that's P&L, not efficiency."

The efficiency argument is table stakes. The P&L argument is that AI expands the decision surface — and every expanded decision surface in Revenue has a price tag attached to it.
